The story so far

Cricket recorded in Sutton since 1832

Sutton Cricket Club traditionally records its foundation as 1877, a date still used on the club badge. However, historical cricket research records a match involving Sutton and Mepal in September 1832, followed by further Sutton matches in 1833. This establishes earlier cricket activity in the village, but does not by itself prove that today's club has operated continuously since 1832.

Local recollections suggest an earlier playing field beside Ely Road, on the right heading out of Sutton towards Witcham Toll, close to the village. The exact site and date of any move to the present Station Road ground have not yet been verified. We are investigating whether 1877 represents the club’s formation, a change of ground, or another milestone.

Our early-match leads come from A History of Cambridgeshire Cricket, which cites contemporary newspapers. We welcome further original records and corrections.

1832

Early Sutton cricket recorded

Historical research identifies a Sutton v Mepal match on 26 September 1832, with Mepal winning by 13 runs. The source cites the Huntingdon, Bedford & Peterborough Gazette of 29 September 1832.

1833

More village fixtures

Further match references include games involving Ely, St Ives and Mepal, showing cricket activity around Sutton well before the traditional founding year.

Traditional club founding year

1877 appears on the club badge and remains the recognised founding year. We are researching whether it marks the formation of the present club or a move to Station Road.

Memories of the ground

Our changing pavilions

According to club recollections, an earlier Sutton CC pavilion was badly damaged when a large roller ran into it. Stan Darby, who was Club President at the time, generously donated a replacement pavilion for the club.

Club recollections suggest that the original pavilion was damaged by a large roller around 1973–74. These dates are approximate and have not yet been confirmed by contemporary records.

That donated pavilion remained in use until the present pavilion opened around 2000. The exact dates of the roller incident, Stan Darby's donation and the new pavilion opening are still being researched.

We would love to hear from anyone who has photographs, documents or memories of any of the club's pavilions, particularly the original structure and the opening of the current building.
